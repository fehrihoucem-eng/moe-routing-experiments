So: is a single global prefetch policy the wrong design?

Yes — but not in the shape you guessed. The two horizons don't rank the same way at every depth; they trade places. At K=8:

L1–L5
Cross-layer wins, and by a lot — 36.6% vs 24.5%. Early routing barely persists between tokens, which matches Mixtral finding layer-0 repetition at chance.
L6–L31
Persistence wins almost throughout — 42.1% vs 37.9%, peaking at 49.3% at L31. Copying the previous token beats an extra layer of hindsight across the whole middle of the stack.
L32–L39
Cross-layer wins again — 44.9% vs 37.7%. Persistence collapses to 26.1% at L39 while cross-layer still holds 47.4% there: the last layer is determined by the layer below it, not by what the previous token did.

An oracle that picks the better horizon per layer scores —, against — for the best global one. That +3.6pp is larger than the +1.6pp that the full mixed predictor buys over free persistence — so specialising the policy by depth is worth more than a better predictor.

Before this goes any further

  • Random is the floor, and popularity is barely above it. Coverage@8 of 9.2% against 3.1% for random — a factor of 2.9. Routing here is close to balanced, so any scheme resting on a static hot set (colibri's HEAT_FILE included) starts from almost nothing.
  • The learned previous-token Markov is worse than just copying at every budget up to 8 — 33.0% vs 38.9%, consistent in all 5 folds. The transition table spreads mass over experts that often follow and loses the identity structure that dominates at small K. Above K=8 it overtakes, because copying only ever has 8 experts to name.
  • Gate weighting is real but tiny: +0.63pp at K=8, positive in all 5 folds. Worth taking; not worth arguing about.
  • The test split has not been read. Everything here is cross-validated inside train, because comparing predictors is itself a modelling decision. No HMM, no sequence model, and no latency measurement — all 10,240 experts fit in this box's VRAM, so every prefetch implication is arithmetic, not observation.
